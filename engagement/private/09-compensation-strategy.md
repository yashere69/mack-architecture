**Two kinds of work, so two kinds of payment.** Setup is front-loaded and finite. The ongoing work is small but continuous, and it is where the results compound.

## 2. Structures compared

| Structure | Good for | Problem for this engagement | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stipend-style** (a fixed monthly amount, loosely defined) | Internships and learning roles inside a firm | Signals "junior helper", not "responsible for the studio's infrastructure". No defined scope, so scope creep is guaranteed. The heavy setup months get underpaid. May read as an employment relationship. | **Avoid** (unless you genuinely want an internship-type arrangement) |
| **Monthly retainer only** | Purely ongoing work | Setup months are 3–5× the effort of later months. Either you are underpaid early or the studio overpays later and starts to question it. | Not on its own |
| **Milestone-only** | Defined projects with an end | SEO, maintenance and reporting don't end. You'd have to renegotiate every month. | Not on its own |
| **Setup fee + monthly maintenance** | Most small-business digital work | Works, but a single upfront setup fee can feel heavy to the client | Good |
| **Hybrid: setup paid by milestone + monthly retainer after launch** | Exactly this: visible deliverables early, continuous work later | — | **Recommended** |

### Recommended structure

1. **Implementation (M1–M4): a fixed fee, paid in three milestone-linked parts.** For example:
   - 40% at kickoff (covers M1),
   - 30% when the site is live on the domain (M2 complete),
   - 30% when the Business Profile is verified and the first batch of project pages is live (M3 + M4 substantially complete).
   Each payment matches something the studio can see. That is the strongest trust signal you have.
2. **Ongoing care (M5 + M6 + maintenance): a monthly retainer** starting the month after launch. Paid at the start of each month. Minimum 3 months, then month-to-month with 30 days' notice either way.
3. **Third-party costs** (domain, Workspace licences, hosting plan, NAS/backup, any paid tool): **paid directly by MACK, from MACK's accounts, at actual cost.** No markup, nothing in your name. This also makes your fee look like exactly what it is: payment for your work.
4. **Extras** (a new studio location, extra pages, large file migrations): a small fixed amount per item, agreed before you start.
5. **What's not included** (say it once): paid ads, social media posting, photography, legal advice.

### Plan B: if Ayushi strongly prefers one monthly number

Offer a **12-month engagement at a flat monthly amount** that spreads the setup across the year, with the same written scope and milestones. Make it clear that if it ends early, the remaining setup portion is settled. Simpler for her, fair for you.

## 3. How to arrive at your numbers (no bluffing)

I don't have reliable market-rate data for freelance digital work in Mumbai, so I won't invent a benchmark. Use this method:

1. **Choose your hourly rate** (the rate you can defend for responsible, documented work at your current skill level).
2. **Setup fee ≈ setup hours × rate.** Use the middle of the range (≈ 50 h) and add 10–15% buffer for waiting, follow-ups and rework.
3. **Monthly ≈ monthly hours × rate** (≈ 8 h).
4. **Sanity-check with the market:** ask 2–3 Mumbai freelancers or small agencies what they'd quote for "website launch + Google Business Profile + analytics + monthly SEO reporting for a small studio". Look at a couple of freelance platforms for comparable scopes. Position yourself where your experience honestly sits.

| Illustrative rate (example only, not a benchmark) | Setup (≈ 50 h + 10%) | Monthly (≈ 8 h) |
|---|---|---|
| ₹500 / h | ≈ ₹27,500 | ≈ ₹4,000 |
| ₹1,000 / h | ≈ ₹55,000 | ≈ ₹8,000 |
| ₹1,500 / h | ≈ ₹82,500 | ≈ ₹12,000 |

**Rules for yourself**
- Decide your number **before** the meeting, plus the lowest you'll accept (your walk-away). Never improvise a number on the spot.
- **If the price comes down, scope comes down too.** Defer M5 (authority), or reduce M4 to the 5 strongest projects. Don't do the same work for less.
- Don't apologise for the number. Say it, then stop talking.
- If you'd do it for a lower amount to build a portfolio piece, say so openly ("as an early project for me"), and get something in return: a case study and a referral/testimonial. Never present a discount as your "normal" rate.

## 4. Paperwork (simple, but in writing)

After the meeting, send a one-to-two-page written summary (email or PDF) covering:
- scope (the scope matrix, condensed), milestones, what's excluded;
- fee, payment schedule, how third-party costs are handled;
- account ownership (everything belongs to MACK; access is removed at the end);
- response times (e.g. issues acknowledged within 1 working day; site-down issues the same day);
- change requests (anything outside scope is quoted first);
- notice period; confidentiality (client details, unpublished projects).

**Tax/admin (check with a CA, not with me):** whether you need GST registration depends on your annual turnover against the threshold. The studio may deduct TDS on professional fees. Ask how they usually pay freelancers (invoice format, bank transfer, timeline).

## 5. Reading the room

- If she asks "**What would an agency charge?**", be honest: "Agencies usually charge more because they have overheads. You'd work directly with me, and I'm responsible for the whole thing."
- If she asks "**Can you guarantee results?**": "I can guarantee the work, the timeline and honest reporting. Nobody can honestly guarantee Google rankings, and Google itself says no one can pay for better ranking."
- If she says "**Let's start with just the website and email**": fine. Price M1 + M2 alone, and propose M3 as the immediate next step, since the Maps listing issue affects how the studio appears to anyone who searches.
- If she offers a **stipend**: thank her, then reframe (see script). Responsibility for accounts, launch and reporting is better served by defined deliverables.

## 6. Meeting flow (presenting the deck)

1. **Before:** rehearse once with the speaker notes. Keep the deck to 30–35 minutes. Have the PDF ready in case the projector misbehaves.
2. **Slide 2 (Starting point):** state facts neutrally. On the Maps listing, *ask*, don't assume: "How would you like the studio to appear on Google Maps? Right now the map link opens a listing named Karan Desai, Architecture + Design." Then listen.
3. **The title "architect":** don't raise the Architects Act in the meeting unless it comes up naturally. Raise it privately later when choosing the Business Profile category: "For the Google category 'Architect', I just need to confirm the principal's Council of Architecture registration."
4. **Slides 17–18 (expectations, next step):** slow down here. This is where trust is built.
5. **After the deck:** confirm scope first ("Does this match what you had in mind? Anything to add or remove?"), *then* move to the money conversation (script in file 10).
6. **Within 24 hours:** send the written summary (§4).
